### 3. FOCUS on Vocabulary — Confusable Words (`1.3`, pp. 54–55)
1. **`nudge` vs. `push` vs. `motivate`:**
   - **nudge** (يدفع أو يحفز بلطف): a gentle encouragement to make someone do something without force (*The teacher nudged the students to read more books*).
   - **push** (يدفع بقوة): physical force or strong pressure.
   - **motivate** (يحفز): to make someone eager to work hard for a goal.
2. **`resilience` vs. `perseverance` vs. `determination` vs. `adaptability`:**
   - **resilience** (المرونة / القدرة على التعافي): recovering quickly from difficulties or failure.
   - **perseverance** (المثابرة): continuing to work despite difficulties without giving up.
   - **determination** (العزيمة / الإصرار): firm resolve to achieve a specific goal.
   - **adaptability** (القدرة على التكيف): adjusting easily to new conditions or environments.
3. **`policy` vs. `politics` vs. `political` vs. `politician`:**
   - **policy** (سياسة / نهج): a set of rules or plans adopted by an organization or government (*a new health policy*).
   - **politics** (مجال وعلم السياسة): activities associated with governing, parties, and elections.
   - **political** (سياسي — صفة): relating to politics (*political issues*).
   - **politician** (رجل سياسة — اسم شخص).
4. **`demonstrate` vs. `explain` vs. `illustrate`:**
   - **demonstrate** (يثبت / يوضح بالتجربة أو الدليل): to prove a fact or show how something works with evidence or action.
   - **explain** (يشرح / يفسر): to give details so someone understands an idea clearly.
   - **illustrate** (يوضح بأمثلة أو صور): to clarify an idea using examples, pictures, or diagrams.

### 4. Reading Text Summary (`1.3`: *"Modern Health: Perspectives on Responsibility and Well-Being"*, pp. 51–52)
The article compares **three perspectives** on who is responsible for healthy living:
1. **The Case for Personal Responsibility:** Emphasizes individual **agency** (capacity to choose and act) and an **"internal locus of control"** (believing one's own actions determine outcomes). Based on **Albert Bandura's Self-Efficacy Theory** (*Journal of Health Psychology*), people with high **self-efficacy** are significantly more likely to maintain healthy habits regardless of socioeconomic background, whereas blaming external forces is **disempowering**.
2. **Social and Environmental Influences (WHO Social Determinants of Health):** Sociologists argue that where we grow up, family income, and **structural barriers** (e.g., neighborhoods with no safe parks or affordable sports facilities, limited income making fresh vegetables harder to prioritize over cheap processed foods, plus billion-dollar junk-food advertising targeting **impressionable** youth) restrict genuine choice.
3. **The Role of Government and Shared Responsibility (Thaler & Sunstein's Nudge Theory):** Responsibility is not an **either-or choice**; it is **shared** among individuals, families, schools, and governments. Economist **Richard Thaler's concept of the "nudge"** (e.g., taxes on high-sugar drinks, restrictions on junk-food ads near schools, national physical activity campaigns) reshapes the environment so the healthier choice becomes the easier choice without removing individual freedom.

### 2. Listening Text Summary (`1.4`: *"2023 Egypt Nationwide School Health Survey"*, p. 67)
- Gathered data from **450 teenagers (aged 14–17)** across urban and rural schools in **five Egyptian governorates**:
  - **68% of participants** reported moderate-to-high stress during exam periods.
  - **Top 4 causes of stress:** (1) Academic pressure (**82%**), (2) Social comparison (**61%**), (3) Insufficient sleep (**57%**), (4) Family expectations (**49%**).
  - **Physical activity impact:** Teens doing at least **30 minutes of exercise 4+ times per week** reported **34% lower stress levels** than sedentary peers, even after controlling for academic performance variables.
  - **Cultural background:** Teens from **collectivist** families sought emotional support from parents/siblings, while teens from **individualistic** households used solo strategies (music, exercising alone, nature). Neither was universally better; **availability and quality of support networks** were the decisive variables.
  - **Urban–Rural Gap:** **74% of urban schools** offered school counseling services, compared to only **31% in rural schools**.

## Part 3: Section B Grammar Focus — Hedging, Conditionals & `If` Alternatives (pp. 58–61 & 72–73)

### 1. Why Use Conditionals & Modals in Academic Writing? (**Hedging / التحوط اللغوي**)
In academic writing, you should not sound 100% certain unless you have unquestionable proof. **Hedging** uses cautious language (`may, might, could, tend to` and `If` conditional structures) to avoid overgeneralizations:
- *If students use social media a lot, they **may** suffer from loneliness.*

### 2. The Four Conditional Types (`Zero, First, Second, Third`)
| Type | `If` Clause | Main Clause | Usage & Special Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Zero Conditional** | `If / When + Present Simple` | `Present Simple` | • **General truths, scientific facts, and regular habits:** *If/When water reaches $100^\circ\text{C}$, it boils.*<br>• **Trap:** If tied to a specific time (`tonight, tomorrow, yesterday`) or a specific instance (*this water*), use 1st/2nd/3rd Conditional instead! |
| **First Conditional** | `If + Present Simple` | `will / may / can / should / have to + inf.` OR **Imperative** (`inf. / Don't + inf.`) | • Real future possibilities, promises, hopes, threats, or polite requests.<br>• *If you meet Omar, **give** him this letter.* |
| **Second Conditional** | `If + Past Simple` | `would / might / could + inf.` | • Unreal/unlikely situations in the **present or future**, or giving advice (*If I **were** you, I wouldn't waste my time* — `were` can be used with all pronouns). |
| **Third Conditional** | `If + Past Perfect (had + P.P.)` | `would / might / could + have + P.P.` | • Unreal past situations (regret/imagining a different past outcome): *If I **had studied** harder, I **would have passed** the test.* |

### 3. `If` Alternatives & Inversions (`بدائل وتركيبات حذف If`, pp. 72–73)

| Alternative / Structure | Formula | Equivalent & Usage |
|---|---|---|
| **`provided (that)` / `providing (that)` / `on condition (that)` / `as long as`** | `+ Clause (Subject + Verb)` | Replaces `If` in affirmative conditionals (means *بشرط أن / طالما*). |
| **`Unless`** | `Unless + Affirmative Verb` | `= If ... not` (the verb immediately after `unless` MUST be affirmative!). |
| **`In case of` / `In the event of`** | `+ Noun / V-ing` | `= If + Subject + Verb` (*In case of an emergency, call this number*). |
| **`Without` / `But for`** | `+ Noun / V-ing` | `= If it weren't for` (2nd Cond.) OR `= If it hadn't been for` (3rd Cond.):<br>*But for the doctor's quick action, the patient would have died.* |
| **`in case` + Clause** | `Action 1 + in case + Subject + Verb` | Means **خشية أن / تحسبًا لـ** (doing something **in advance** to prepare for a possible future problem — NOT interchangeable with `if`!):<br>*Save a copy of the file **in case** the computer crashes.* |
| **Inversion 1: `Should`** | **`Should + Subject + inf.`** | Replaces `If` in **1st and 2nd Conditionals**:<br>***Should** the weather **improve**, we will continue the trip.* |
| **Inversion 2: `Were`** | **`Were + Subject + noun/adj`** OR **`Were + Subject + to + inf.`** | Replaces `If` in **2nd Conditional ONLY**:<br>***Were** she my sister, I would help her.* / ***Were** the company **to reduce** prices, sales would increase.* |
| **Inversion 3: `Had`** | **`Had + Subject + P.P.`** (Negative: `Had + Subject + not + P.P.`) | Replaces `If` in **3rd Conditional**:<br>***Had** they **informed** us earlier, we would have attended.* |
| **`Suppose / Supposing (that) / Imagine (that)`** | `+ Clause, Question Main Clause?` | Used to introduce hypothetical questions (1st, 2nd, or 3rd Cond.):<br>***Imagine that** you won a million pounds, what would you spend it on?* |
