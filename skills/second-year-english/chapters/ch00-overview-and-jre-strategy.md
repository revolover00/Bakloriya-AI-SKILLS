# Chapter 0: Curriculum Overview & JRE Strategy (English — 2nd Year Baccalaureate 2027)

## 1. Curriculum Philosophy (Egyptian Baccalaureate System — Grade 11 / 2nd Secondary)
Under the new **Egyptian Baccalaureate System (نظام البكالوريا المصرية — الصف الثاني بكالوريا)**, English instruction shifts away from rote memorization and fixed question patterns toward **critical thinking, textual analysis, contextual vocabulary/grammar application, and integrated skills**:

1. **Focus on Skills:** Understanding and applying language rather than memorizing answers.
2. **Critical Thinking Skills:** Moving from remembering to analyzing and making inferences (*Why? How? What can you infer?*).
3. **Reading Skills:** Analyzing texts by examining main ideas, details, relationships, tone, and the writer's purpose.
4. **Vocabulary in Context:** Inferring word meaning, collocations, derivatives, and nuances from context rather than isolated word lists.
5. **Grammar in Context:** Analyzing communicative situations to determine **when and why** a grammatical structure is used (e.g., participial phrases for conciseness, hedging/conditionals to avoid overgeneralization).
6. **Evaluate Ideas and Information:** Evaluating claims, arguments, solutions, and textual evidence using the **JRE Framework**.
7. **Spiral Approach:** Recycling and deepening vocabulary, grammar, and analytical skills across all five concepts/units.

## 3. The JRE Strategy (`Justify – Reason – Evidence` / استراتيجية التبرير – السبب – الدليل)

A central pillar of the 2nd-Year Baccalaureate reading, listening, and writing curriculum is the **JRE Strategy**. Instead of giving an unsupported personal opinion, students must structure every analytical answer into three distinct components:

| Component | Academic Function | Guiding Questions |
|---|---|---|
| **J — Justify**<br>*(التبرير / الادعاء / الرأي)* | **The Claim / Opinion / Judgment:** Your direct, clear answer to the prompt, central thesis, or inference statement. | • *What is the writer trying to say?*<br>• *To what extent do you agree with...?* |
| **R — Reason**<br>*(السبب / التحليل المنطقي)* | **The Analysis / Connection:** The logical explanation of **how or why** your justification works, connecting your claim to cause-and-effect, theme, tone, or structure. | • *Why is this answer true based on context?*<br>• *How does the author's comparison/word choice work?* |
| **E — Evidence**<br>*(الدليل النصي)* | **The Textual Proof:** Direct quotes, specific statistics/numbers, or accurate paraphrases pulled directly from the reading or listening passage. | • *Extract the exact proof from the text.*<br>• *What example/data from the text proves this?* |

### Worked Model Example from the Textbook (`The Global Water Crisis`, pp. 8–9)
- **Prompt:** *Should governments take immediate action to solve the global water crisis today? State your position (Justify), explain why (Reason), and support your answer using specific data from the passage (Evidence).*
  - **Justify (Claim):** Yes, governments must act immediately rather than waiting.
  - **Reason (Logic):** Acting now prevents far more costly and dangerous disasters in the future, such as food shortages, widespread illness, and violent conflicts over shared water.
  - **Evidence (Fact from Text):** The passage shows that **2.2 billion people** already lack safe water, **70% of fresh water** is used by farms, and **21 of the 37 largest underground sources** are being used up faster than they refill.

- **Section A `3.1` (p. 178 — Karnak Temple Complex):**
  - **Q1:** **J:** Yes, I agree. **R:** The complex was built and changed over more than two thousand years by different rulers. **E:** The text says that Karnak developed *"from the reign of Senusret in the Middle Kingdom to the Ptolemaic era,"* with each pharaoh adding, changing, or inscribing something.
