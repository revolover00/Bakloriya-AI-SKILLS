## Section 1: Story Text and Literary Analysis — Chapters 6 to 8

### Chapter Summaries

**Chapter 6: Malone's Night Adventure**
Edward Malone undertakes a dangerous solo adventure at night on the plateau. He faces the darkness, strange sounds, and the constant threat of prehistoric beasts. This adventure tests his courage and proves to his companions that he is more than just a journalist — he is brave and reliable.

**Chapter 7: Prisoners of the Ape-Men**
The explorers are captured by a tribe of fierce ape-like creatures who live in caves on the plateau. These ape-men are violent, primitive, and at war with the human tribe (the Accala) who also live on the plateau. The explorers must find a way to escape before the ape-men harm them.

**Chapter 8: The Battle for the Plateau**
Professor Challenger, Lord John Roxton, Summerlee, and Malone join forces with the Accala tribe to fight the ape-men. Using guns and strategy, the explorers help the Accala win the battle and take control of the plateau. This is the climax of the conflict on the plateau.

### Character Development

| Character | Role in Chapters 6–8 | Change |
|-----------|---------------------|--------|
| **Edward Malone** | Goes on a night adventure; fights beside the Accala | Grows from an unsure reporter into a brave, trusted companion |
| **Professor Challenger** | Leads the fight with courage and knowledge | Shows leadership and bravery, earning respect |
| **Lord John Roxton** | Fights with skill and experience; protects the group | Acts as the experienced hunter and protector |
| **Professor Summerlee** | Initially fearful; joins the battle | Begins to respect Challenger more after seeing his courage |
| **The Ape-Men** | Antagonists; capture the explorers and war with the Accala | Represent primitive danger and the wild plateau |

### Literary Themes

- **Courage under pressure:** Malone's night adventure shows that courage is action despite fear.
- **Cooperation across cultures:** The explorers and the Accala tribe cooperate to defeat a common enemy.
- **Civilization vs. savagery:** The battle between the human Accala tribe and the ape-men explores the line between human and beast.
- **Science and survival:** Challenger's knowledge helps the group survive in a prehistoric world.

### [JRE] Question 7 — Justify, Reasoning & Evidence — Is Malone's night adventure important to his character development?

**Justify:** Yes. The night adventure is the turning point where Malone proves he is brave and reliable.

**Reasoning:** Before the adventure, Malone wants adventure mainly to impress Gladys. During the night adventure, he acts despite fear, showing real courage. This changes how his companions see him.

**Evidence:** Malone goes alone into the dark plateau at night in Chapter 6. In Chapter 8, he fights beside the Accala, and his companions treat him as a trusted comrade.

**Step-by-Step Solution:**
Step 1: Recall Malone's actions in Chapter 6 (night adventure).
Step 2: Identify why this matters (proves courage).
Step 3: Provide reasoning (real courage is acting despite fear).
Step 4: Cite evidence (solo night adventure; fighting beside the Accala).

### [JRE] Question 8 — Justify, Reasoning & Evidence — Is the battle for the plateau mainly about survival or about exploration?

**Justify:** It is mainly about survival. The explorers and the Accala fight to protect themselves and their homes from the ape-men.

**Reasoning:** Exploration is the reason they came to the plateau, but the battle happens because their lives are in danger. Survival becomes the priority.

**Evidence:** In Chapter 8, the explorers take up arms to defend the Accala village and themselves against the ape-men's attack. The goal is to defeat the enemy and stay alive.

**Step-by-Step Solution:**
Step 1: Recall the purpose of the battle in Chapter 8.
Step 2: Identify whether it is exploration or survival (survival).
Step 3: Provide reasoning (lives are in danger; fighting to protect home).
Step 4: Cite evidence (armed battle with the ape-men; defense of the Accala village).
