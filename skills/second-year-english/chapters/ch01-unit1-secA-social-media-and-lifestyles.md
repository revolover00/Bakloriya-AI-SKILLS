### 4. FOCUS on Vocabulary — Confusable Words (`1.1`)
1. **`phenomenon` vs. `trend` vs. `occurrence`:**
   - **phenomenon** (ظاهرة): an observable fact or situation that is remarkable or needs explanation (*Social media addiction is a modern phenomenon*).
   - **trend** (اتجاه / موضة رائجة): a general direction in which something is developing or becoming popular (*Online learning is a growing trend*).
   - **occurrence** (حدوث / واقعة): the mere fact of something happening (*The occurrence of earthquakes is common there*).
2. **`acquaintances` vs. `friends` vs. `colleagues` vs. `companions`:**
   - **acquaintances** (معارف): people you know casually or superficially, not close friends (*I have hundreds of acquaintances on social media*).
   - **friends** (أصدقاء): people you have a strong bond and mutual trust with.
   - **colleagues** (زملاء عمل أو دراسة): people you share a workplace or study environment with.
   - **companions** (رفقاء / مرافقون): people you spend time with on a trip, hobby, or shared situation (*traveling companions*).
3. **`evaluate` vs. `assess` vs. `judge` vs. `estimate`:**
   - **evaluate** (يقيّم): to form an idea of the value or quality of something after careful examination.
   - **assess** (يقيّم / يقدّر رسميًا): to measure the level, condition, or needs, often officially (*assess the patient's condition / assess damage*).
   - **judge** (يحكم على): to form an opinion or decision based on what you see or know, sometimes personally (*Don't judge a person by appearance*).
   - **estimate** (يقدّر / يحسب تقريبًا): to roughly calculate a value, number, or cost without complete data (*We estimate the project will take three months*).
4. **`sensitive` vs. `sensible`:**
   - **sensitive** (حساس / سريع التأثر): easily affected emotionally or physically, or able to notice small changes (*Maged is very sensitive*).
   - **sensible** (عاقل / حكيم / منطقي): showing good judgment and making practical decisions (*It is sensible to save money*).

- **Reading Text (*"The Impossible Standard: How Social Media Is Reshaping the Way Teenagers See Themselves"*):**

  - Over the past decade, social media platforms have transformed from simple communication tools into carefully chosen **stages** where individuals perform constructed versions of their lives.
  - A **2023 American Psychological Association (APA)** study found that teenagers who spent **more than 3 hours daily** on image-based platforms (Instagram, TikTok) were **twice as likely** to report symptoms of low self-esteem and body dissatisfaction compared to those who used social media for less than 1 hour per day.

### 4. Reading Text Summary (`1.2`: *"Habits for a Healthy Lifestyle"*, pp. 30–31)
1. **Exercise and Physical Activity:** Health experts recommend **at least 60 minutes** of moderate-to-hard physical activity daily (cycling, swimming, brisk walk). It releases **endorphins** (reducing stress/anxiety, boosting mood and self-confidence) and improves sleep and school concentration. Spending face-to-face **quality time** with family and friends builds emotional **resilience**, and **mindful consumption** means being conscious of both the food we eat and the media/information we absorb daily.

## Part 3: Section A Grammar Focus (pp. 21–23 & 36–39)

### Grammar 1: Participial Phrases (`العبارات الوصفية باستخدام اسم الفاعل أو المفعول`)
Participial phrases begin with a participle (`V-ing`, `P.P.`, or `Having + P.P.`) to pack more information into a single sentence concisely.

#### 1. Present Participle Phrase (`V-ing` — اسم الفاعل للمبني للمعلوم)
**Condition:** The subject of the participial phrase MUST be the **same as the subject of the main clause**, and the action is **Active**:
- **Replacing a Relative Clause (`who/which/that + active verb`):**
  - *Students **who work** hard usually succeed.* $\rightarrow$ *Students **working** hard usually succeed.*
- **Replacing a Reason Clause (`because / as / since`):**
  - *Because Ahmed felt tired, he went to bed early.* $\rightarrow$ ***Feeling** tired, Ahmed went to bed early.*
- **Replacing a Time Clause (`while / when`):**
  - *While he was walking home, he saw an accident.* $\rightarrow$ ***Walking** home, he saw an accident.*
- **Replacing a Conditional Clause (`if` in active voice):**
  - *If you take this medicine, you will recover quickly.* $\rightarrow$ ***Taking** this medicine, you will recover quickly.*
- **Expressing Result at the end of a sentence (replacing `and + verb`):**
  - *The storm destroyed many houses, **leaving** many people homeless.*
- **Negation:** Place `Not` directly before the participle: ***Not wanting** to miss the bus, they ran to the bus stop.*

#### 2. Past Participle Phrase (`P.P.` — اسم المفعول للمبني للمجهول)
Used when the verb is **Passive**:
- **Replacing a Passive Relative Clause (`who/which/that + v. to be + P.P.`):**
  - *The car **which was damaged** in the accident was repaired.* $\rightarrow$ *The car **damaged** in the accident was repaired.*
- **Replacing a Passive `If` Clause:**
  - *If this medicine is taken three times a day, it will have a good effect.* $\rightarrow$ ***Taken** three times a day, this medicine will have a good effect.*
- **Replacing a Passive Reason Clause (`because / since / as`):**
  - *Because she was bitten by a scorpion, she was carried to hospital at once.* $\rightarrow$ ***Bitten** by a scorpion, she was carried to hospital at once.*

#### 3. Perfect Participle Phrase (`Having + P.P.` / Passive: `Having been + P.P.`)
Used instead of `after` or `because` when **Action 1 finished completely before Action 2**:
- **Active:** *After he had finished his homework, he watched TV.* $\rightarrow$ ***Having finished** his homework, he watched TV.*
- **Passive:** *After the house had been cleaned, it was decorated.* $\rightarrow$ ***Having been cleaned**, the house was decorated.*
- **Negation:** `Not having + P.P.` (*Not having studied well, he failed the test*).

> **⚠️ Critical Exam Pitfall (Dangling Participle):**
> Never use a participial phrase if the two clauses have **different subjects**!
> - ❌ *Walking down the street, the trees looked beautiful.* (Trees cannot walk!)
> - ✅ *Walking down the street, **I** saw beautiful trees.*

### Grammar 2: Relative Clauses (`عبارات الوصل`, pp. 36–39)

| Relative Pronoun | Usage & Structure | Key Exam Notes & Restrictions |
|---|---|---|
| **`who / that`** | Human subject or object (`اسم عاقل + who/that + verb OR subject + verb`) | `that` is **NEVER** used after a comma (in non-defining clauses) or after a preposition. |
| **`whom`** | Human object (`اسم عاقل + whom + subject + verb`) | Can be preceded by a preposition: *Marwan, **with whom** I go to the club, is my neighbor.* (`who/that` cannot follow a preposition!). |
| **`which / that`** | Non-human subject or object (`اسم غير عاقل + which/that`) | • `which` can follow a preposition (*the house **in which** I lived*).<br>• `which` can refer to an **entire preceding clause**: *Menna quarreled with her brother, **which** made their father angry.* |
| **`where`** | Place (`المكان + where + subject + verb`) = `in/at which` | Use `where` ONLY if it means *"in which"* and no preposition appears with the verb. Use `which` if the place is the subject/object or has a preposition (*The house **which** I bought* / *The house **which** I lived **in***). |
| **`when`** | Time (`الزمان + when + subject + verb`) = `in/on/at which` | *Summer, **when** (`= in which`) we travel, is my favorite season.* |
| **`whose`** | Possession for humans & non-humans (`اسم + whose + noun`) | • Replaces `his/her/its/their/my/'s`.<br>• Can follow a preposition (*The man **in whose house** we live*).<br>• Watch out for words that can be **both verbs and nouns** (`change, comfort, control, copy, cure, dream, dress, drink, drive, email, end, fight, function, hand, hope, influence, interest, mistake, need, offer, play, promise, smell, smoke, stay, struggle, surprise, talk, visit, walk, watch, work, worry`): *I like the stories **whose ends** are happy.* |
| **`what`** | Means **`the thing(s) that`** (has NO antecedent noun before it!) | `فعل/ضمير + what + فاعل + فعل` (*I didn't understand **what** he had said* / ***What** makes me angry is that...*). |

- **Defining vs. Non-Defining Relative Clauses:**
  - *My sister **who** lives in Alex came to visit us.* (No commas = Defining $\rightarrow$ I have **more than one sister**, specifying which one).
  - *My sister, **who** lives in Alex, came to visit us.* (Commas = Non-defining $\rightarrow$ I have **only one sister**; extra information).
- **`who's` vs. `whose`:**
  - `who's + V-ing / noun / adj` = `who is` (*The boy who's running*).
  - `who's + P.P.` = `who has` (*The boy who's finished his homework*).
  - `whose + Noun` = possession (*The boy whose phone was stolen*).
