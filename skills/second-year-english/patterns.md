# Grammar, Vocabulary & Writing Patterns — 2nd Year Baccalaureate English (Term 1 — 2027)

This reference provides step-by-step diagnostic and problem-solving patterns for every question type in the **2nd-year Egyptian Baccalaureate English** exam: **Multiple-Choice Vocabulary & Grammar**, **Sentence Rewrite (`Use the word in brackets`)**, **Cloze Text Completion**, **Reading Comprehension**, **JRE Critical Thinking**, and **Essay / Paragraph / Diary / Leaflet Writing**.

---

## Pattern 1: Participial Phrases & Relative Clauses (Unit 1A)

### Step-by-Step Diagnostic:
1. **Check the voice of the reduced relative or adverbial clause:**
   - **Active (`الفاعل هو من قام بالفعل`) $\rightarrow$ Present Participle (`V-ing`):**
     - *Students **who use** social media daily* $\rightarrow$ *Students **using** social media daily...*
   - **Passive (`الفاعل وقع عليه الفعل`) $\rightarrow$ Past Participle (`P.P.`):**
     - *Photos **which are posted** online* $\rightarrow$ *Photos **posted** online...*
   - **Sequence with emphasis on completion before the second action $\rightarrow$ `Having + P.P.` (Active) or `Having been + P.P.` (Passive):**
     - ***Having finished** his homework, he went to bed.*
     - ***Having been warned** twice, he was fired.*
2. **Preventing Dangling Participles:** The subject immediately after the comma **MUST** be the subject of the participial phrase!
   - $\checkmark$ *Walking down the street, **I** saw an old friend.* ($\times$ *Walking down the street, the trees looked beautiful.*)
3. **Relative Pronoun Selection:**
   - **Preposition immediately before the pronoun:** Use **`whom`** (for people) or **`which`** (for things). **NEVER** use `that` or `who` directly after a preposition!
   - **After commas (Non-defining clause), `all, some, many, much, none + of`:** Use **`which`** (things) or **`whom`** (people) — **NEVER** `that`.
   - **Place (`المكان`):** Use **`where`** (`= in/at which`) ONLY when an action happens inside it and there is no preposition; use **`which`** if the place is treated as an object, takes a preposition (`which ... in`), or is followed directly by a verb!

## Pattern 2: Hedging, Conditionals (`0, 1, 2, 3`) & `If` Alternatives / Inversions (Unit 1B)

### 1. Identifying the Conditional Type:
| Type | `If`-Clause | Main Clause | Key Signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Zero (`0`)** | Present Simple | Present Simple | Scientific law, general truth, or `when + habit` |
| **First (`1`)** | Present Simple | `will / can / may / should + inf.` or Imperative | Real future possibility, warning, promise |
| **Second (`2`)** | Past Simple (`were` for all) | `would / could / might + inf.` | Imaginary present or advice (`If I were you...`) |
| **Third (`3`)** | Past Perfect (`had + P.P.`) | `would / could / might + have + P.P.` | Regret/imaginary past (opposite of what happened) |

### 2. High-Yield `If` Alternatives & Formal Inversions:
- **`Unless + Affirmative Verb`** `= If ... not`.
- **`In case + Clause`** (تحسبًا لـ — نأخذ الاحتياط **قبل** الحدث) vs. **`If / Provided that / As long as / On condition that + Clause`** (بشرط أن).
- **`In case of + Noun / V-ing`** (`= If there is`); **`Without / But for + Noun / V-ing`** `= If it weren't for + Noun` (Type 2) `= If it hadn't been for + Noun` (Type 3).
- **Three Formal Inversions (Drop `if`):**
  1. **Type 1 (`Should`):** **`Should + Subj + inf.,`** `will / Imperative...`
  2. **Type 2 (`Were`):** **`Were + Subj + Noun/Adj`** OR **`Were + Subj + to + inf.,`** `would + inf.`
  3. **Type 3 (`Had`):** **`Had + Subj + P.P.,`** `would have + P.P.`

## Pattern 3: Gerunds (`V-ing`) vs. Infinitives (`to + inf.` / Bare `inf.`) (Unit 2A & 2B)

### 1. Verbs with Meaning Shifts (`فرق المعنى الحاسم`):
| Verb | `+ V-ing` (Gerund) | `+ to + inf.` (Infinitive) |
|---|---|---|
| **`remember` / `forget`** | Action happened **FIRST**, then remembered/forgotten (*I remember locking the door*). | Remembered/forgot the duty **FIRST**, then acted (*Remember to lock the door*). |
| **`regret`** | Remorse over a **past** action (*I regret wasting my time*). | Polite announcement of bad news **now** (*We regret to inform you...*). |
| **`stop`** | Quit the activity completely (*stopped smoking*). | Paused one action **in order to** do another (*stopped to buy water*). |
| **`try`** | Experiment with an easy method to see what happens (*Try restarting the phone*). | Make a hard effort at something difficult (*tried to lift the heavy box*). |
| **`go on`** | Continue the **same** action (*went on talking*). | Move to a **new** stage/topic (*went on to discuss solutions*). |
| **`mean`** | Entail / involve (`يتضمن/يستلزم`: *Success means working hard*). | Intend (`يقصد/ينوي`: *I didn't mean to hurt you*). |
| **`need` / `require` / `want`** | **Passive meaning** with an inanimate subject (*The car needs washing `= needs to be washed`*). | Active meaning (*I need to wash the car*). |

### 2. Expressions Ending in Preposition `to` + **`V-ing`** (Exam Trap!):
- **`look forward to`**, **`be/get used to`**, **`be accustomed to`**, **`object to`**, **`lead to`**, **`contribute to`**, **`in addition to`**, **`owing to`**, **`devote/dedicate ... to`**, **`be committed to`** $\rightarrow$ **ALL take `V-ing` or a Noun!**
- **Perception Verbs (`see, hear, watch, notice`):**
  - Active `+ Object + V-ing` (saw **part** of the action in progress) vs. `+ Object + bare inf.` (saw the **whole** action from start to finish).
  - **In the Passive, bare infinitive ALWAYS becomes `to + inf.`:** *He was seen **to enter** the building.* (`except let` $\rightarrow$ *was allowed to* or *was let go*).
- **`too + adj/adv + to + inf.`** (سلبي: أكثر مما ينبغي فلا يمكن فعل الشيء) vs. **`adj/adv + enough + to + inf.`** (إيجابي: بالقدر الكافي).

## Pattern 4: Prepositional Phrases of Place & Time + Concession Clauses (Unit 3A & 3B)

### 1. Time & Place Preposition Traps:
- **`since + starting point`** (*since 2020 / since childhood*) vs. **`for + duration`** (*for two thousand years*) vs. **`during + noun name of period/event`** (*during the reign of Ramesses II / during the holiday*) vs. **`throughout + entire period uninterrupted`** (*rained throughout the night*).
- **`by + deadline`** (بحلول — في هذا الموعد أو قبله: *finish by 12 o'clock*) vs. **`until + endpoint of continued state`** (تستمر الحالة حتى: *open until 7 p.m.*) vs. **`within + time span`** (في غضون: *within three months*).
- **`before / after` Part-of-Speech Question:**
  - Followed by **Noun / `V-ing`** (`After lunch, ...`) $\rightarrow$ **Preposition (`حرف جر`)**.
  - Followed by **Subject + Verb** (`After he ate lunch, ...`) $\rightarrow$ **Conjunction (`أداة ربط`)**.

### 2. Concession Clause Rewrite Formulas:
- **`Although / Even though / Though / While it is true that + Subject + Verb`**
  $\iff$ **`Despite / In spite of + Noun Phrase / V-ing / the fact that + Subject + Verb`**
- **`However + Adjective/Adverb + Subject + Verb, Main Clause`** (`= Although + Subj + Verb + very + Adj/Adv`):
  - *Although he was very courageous, he escaped* $\iff$ ***However courageous he was,** he escaped.*
- **`Whatever + Noun (+ Subj + Verb)`:** *Whatever mistakes he makes (`= Whatever his mistakes`), I will forgive him.*

## Pattern 5: Present Perfect Simple vs. Continuous & The 7 Rewrite Rules (Unit 4A & 4B)

### 1. Present Perfect Simple (`have/has + P.P.`) vs. Continuous (`have/has + been + V-ing`):
- **Use Continuous (`have/has been + V-ing`)** when:
  1. The action is still going on (`all day / all morning / for 3 hours now / since ... still`).
  2. A past prolonged activity explains a visible present symptom (*He is exhausted because he **has been swimming** all morning*).
- **Switch to Simple (`have/has + P.P.`) immediately if:**
  1. **A specific number/amount/frequency is mentioned** (*He **has swum** five kilometers today / I **have drunk** 3 cups of coffee*).
  2. **The verb is a Stative Verb** (`know, own, have [possess], be, love, believe, understand`: *I **have known** him for 10 years*).
  3. **The verb expresses instantaneous completion** (`finish, break, lose, arrive, stop`).

### 2. Quick-Reference Rewrite Table (`Use the word in brackets`):
1. `I haven't visited Alex for a long time.` $\xrightarrow{\text{(since)}}$ **`It's a long time since I last visited Alex.`**
2. `I haven't met Ali since we were in Alex.` $\xrightarrow{\text{(when)}}$ **`I last met Ali when we were in Alex.`**
3. `He is still doing his homework.` $\xrightarrow{\text{(yet)}}$ **`He hasn't finished doing his homework yet.`**
4. `Ramy has lived in Luxor for 10 years.` $\xrightarrow{\text{(started/ago)}}$ **`Ramy started to live in Luxor 10 years ago.`**
5. `I haven't played tennis since 2024.` $\xrightarrow{\text{(The last time)}}$ **`The last time I played tennis was in 2024.`**
6. `Amir repaired his car a moment ago.` $\xrightarrow{\text{(just)}}$ **`Amir has just repaired his car.`**
7. `She has never traveled abroad before.` $\xrightarrow{\text{(ever)}}$ **`It's the first time she has ever traveled abroad.`**

## Pattern 6: Cause/Effect, Modals & Adverbial Word Order (Unit 5A & 5B)

### 1. Cause & Effect Rewrite Rules:
- `Because / Since / As + Subject + Verb` $\iff$ `Because of / Due to / Owing to / On account of + Noun / V-ing`.
- **Same-Subject Participial Reduction of `Because`:**
  - Active: *Because Sara was exhausted, she went to bed* $\xrightarrow{\text{(Being)}}$ ***Being exhausted,** Sara went to bed.*
  - Passive: *Because Ali was punished severely, he didn't return* $\xrightarrow{\text{(Punished)}}$ ***Punished severely,** Ali didn't return.*

### 2. Modal Verb Rewrite Equivalents (p. 393):
- `It is necessary for all visitors to wear ID badges.` $\xrightarrow{\text{(have to / must)}}$ **`All visitors have to / must wear ID badges.`**
- `It is forbidden / prohibited / not allowed to park here.` $\xrightarrow{\text{(mustn't)}}$ **`You mustn't park here.`**
- `It isn't necessary for you to bring lunch.` $\xrightarrow{\text{(don't have to / needn't)}}$ **`You don't have to / needn't bring lunch.`**
- `I advise you (not) to... / It is (in)advisable to...` $\xrightarrow{\text{(should / shouldn't)}}$ **`You should / shouldn't + inf.`**
- `It is possible for + Object + to + inf.` $\xrightarrow{\text{(can)}}$ **`Subject + can + inf.`** *(Ability/Potential!)*
- `It is possible that + Subject + Verb` $\xrightarrow{\text{(may / might)}}$ **`Subject + may / might + inf.`** *(Possibility!)*
- `It was possible for him to go by bus, but he didn't.` $\xrightarrow{\text{(could have)}}$ **`He could have gone by bus.`**

### 3. Adverbial Word-Order Check:
- **Adverb of Degree (`significantly, completely, partially`):** Goes **before** `adj/adv`, **before** a single main verb (`partially funds`), **between** `auxiliary + main verb` (`has significantly reduced` / `will probably arrive`), or at the **very end** (`reduced our reliance completely`) — **NEVER** between a verb and its object!
- **Adverb of Frequency (`always, usually, often, never`):** Goes **before** a main verb (`always studies`), **after** `Verb to Be` (`is always`), or **between** `auxiliary + main verb` (`has always been`).
- **Adverb of Certainty/Stance (`Clearly, Undoubtedly, Fortunately, Surprisingly`):** Starts the sentence followed by a **comma (`,`)**, or stands before the main verb (`will inevitably fail`).
