### 3. Reading Text Summary (`5.1`: *"The Climate Crisis: Understanding Our Burning Planet"* by Dr. Amara Osei, pp. 339–340)
- **Primary Driver:** Scientists agree that **human activity** is the **primary driver** of the climate crisis. Since the **industrial revolution**, burning **fossil fuels (coal, oil, and natural gas)** has **released** billions of tonnes of $\text{CO}_2$, creating the **Greenhouse Effect** (trapping heat).
- **Four Major Consequences:**
  1. **Melting ice & rising seas:** Arctic ice sheets and ancient **glaciers** are melting at an **alarming rate**; sea levels could rise by **up to 1 meter by 2100**, threatening coastal and low-lying island nations.
  2. **Extreme weather events:** **Hurricanes**, wildfires (e.g., in Canada, Greece, Australia in 2023), droughts, and floods are occurring with greater **intensity**.
  3. **Biodiversity loss:** Coral reefs (*"the rainforests of the sea"*, such as the **Great Barrier Reef**) suffer mass **bleaching** due to ocean warming and **acidification**; scientists warn of a **sixth mass extinction**.
  4. **Food security threats:** Once-**fertile** lands are becoming dry; the **world's poorest communities**, who contribute the least to climate change, are hit the hardest.
- **Urgency of Action:** The scientific **consensus** warns that exceeding **$1.5^\circ\text{C}$ above pre-industrial levels** will bring **disastrous and irreversible (unchangeable)** results. Every **fraction** of a degree matters.

### 3. Listening Text & Energy Sources Summary (`5.2`: Podcast with Dr. Layla Hussein, pp. 353–354)
- **Four Main Renewable Energy Sources (p. 353):**
  1. **Solar:** Photovoltaic panels convert sunlight into electricity (**90% cost drop** in the last decade; fastest-growing worldwide; **Benban Solar Park** is Egypt's **flagship** clean energy project).
  2. **Wind:** Turbines **harness kinetic wind energy** (**Denmark** generates over **60%** of its electricity from wind, creating thousands of jobs).

  4. **Geothermal:** Earth's heat powers turbines (24/7 consistent power with very low carbon footprint).

## Part 3: Section A Grammar Focus — Cause & Effect Connectors + Modal Verbs (pp. 345–347 & 359–361)

### 1. Cause and Effect Connectors (`روابط السبب والنتيجة`, pp. 345–347)

| Type | Connectors | Structure & Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Cause + Full Clause** | **`because` / `as` / `since`**<br>**`, for`** | **`+ Subject + Verb`**<br>• *Ahmed was ill **because** he had eaten bad food.* (`because` answers *Why?* and emphasizes an unknown reason).<br>• ***Since / As** it is raining, we will take a taxi.* (`since/as` focus on the result when the cause is already known).<br>• *I believed her**, for** she had never lied to me before.* (`, for` comes **only mid-sentence after a comma**). |
| **2. Cause + Noun / `V-ing`** | **`because of` / `due to` / `owing to` / `on account of` / `as a result of` / `thanks to` / `through`** | **`+ Noun / Adjective + Noun / V-ing`** *(or `+ the fact that + Subj + Verb`)*<br>• ***Because of / Owing to** his illness, he couldn't attend.*<br>• *His absence was **due to** illness.* (`due to` often follows `verb to be`).<br>• ***Thanks to** your design, the booklet looks great.* (positive outcome).<br>• *He achieved top marks **through** hard work and dedication.* |
| **3. Participial Reduction of `Because`** | **`V-ing` (Active)**<br>**`P.P.` (Passive)** | When both clauses share the same subject:<br>• *Because Ali was ill, he couldn't come* $\rightarrow$ ***Being ill,** Ali couldn't come.*<br>• *Because Ali was punished severely, he didn't return* $\rightarrow$ ***Punished severely,** Ali didn't return.* |
| **4. Result Connectors & Verbs** | **`, so`**<br>**`Therefore, / Consequently, / As a result,`**<br>**`; that's why`**<br>**`lead to / result in / cause`** | • *Our team played badly**, so** they lost.*<br>• *Our team played badly**; therefore, / . As a result,** they lost.*<br>• *He didn't study**; that's why** he failed.*<br>• *Releasing greenhouse gases **leads to / results in / causes** extreme temperatures.* |

### 2. Modal Verbs of Necessity, Prohibition, Advice, Ability & Possibility (pp. 359–361 & 392–393)

| Function | Modals & Equivalents | Key Rules & Distinctions |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Necessity (`الضرورة والإلزام`)** | **`must + inf.`** vs. **`have to / has to + inf.`** | • **`must`:** Internal/personal obligation, strong feeling, warm invitation, or general law text (*I promised my mother, so I **must** call her*).<br>• **`have to / has to`:** External obligation imposed by rules, school, boss, or circumstances where you have no choice (*You **have to** wear a tie at this bank*). |
| **2. Lack of Necessity vs. Prohibition** | **`don't / doesn't have to + inf.`** (`= needn't + inf.`)<br>vs.<br>**`mustn't + inf.`** | • **`don't have to / needn't` (ليس ضروريًا — اختياري):** *Food is available inside, so you **don't have to** bring lunch.*<br>• **`mustn't` (ممنوع / محظور / ضد القانون `= It is forbidden / prohibited / not allowed`):** *Visitors **mustn't** touch the ancient objects.* |
| **3. Advice (`النصيحة`)** | **`should / ought to / had better (not) + inf.`** | `= I advise you (not) to... / It is (in)advisable to... / If I were you, I would(n't)...` (*Note: `had better` is strong advice for a specific immediate situation, e.g., Your car is making a strange noise; you **had better** see your mechanic*). |
| **4. Ability (`القدرة والاستطاعة`)** | • **Present:** `can + inf.` `= am/is/are able to + inf.` `= am/is/are capable of + V-ing` `= have/has the ability to + inf.` `= enable + obj + to + inf.`<br>• **Past General Ability:** **`could + inf.`** (*My grandfather **could** swim long distances when he was young*).<br>• **Managed Specific Past Situation:** **`was / were able to + inf.`** (*My rival was strong, but I **was able to** beat him*).<br>• **Unfulfilled Past Ability:** **`could have + P.P.`** (*كان بإمكانه أن يفعل شيئًا في الماضي لكنه لم يفعله: I went on foot, but I **could have gone** by bus*). |
| **5. Possibility (`الاحتمال`)** | • **Present/Future:** `may / might / could + inf.` (`might/could` = weaker chance than `may`; `It is possible that + Subj + will/may` $\neq$ `It is possible for + obj + to + inf.` which means `can`!).<br>• **General/Scientific Fact Possibility:** **`can + inf.`** (*Smoking **can** cause cancer / Computers **can** crash sometimes*).<br>• **Past Possibility:** **`may / might / could + have + P.P.`** (*Perhaps they won = They **may/might have won***).<br>• **Passive Modals:** Present/Future `modal + be + P.P.`; Past `modal + have been + P.P.`. |
