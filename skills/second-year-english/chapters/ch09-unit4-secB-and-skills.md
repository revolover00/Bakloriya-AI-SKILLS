### 3. Listening Text Summary (`4.3`: *"How apps use your time"*, pp. 286–287)
- **"If you are not paying for the product, you are the product":** Every minute spent scrolling, the app collects data (what you like, what you **ignore**, how long you stop on each image) and sells it to advertisers. Your **time and attention** are being bought and sold.
- **Engagement Design:** Features like **infinite scroll** (*where the feed never ends*) and **autoplay** (*where the next video starts automatically*) were built intentionally by teams of designers to keep users online as long as possible. By **age 17**, the average teenager has spent more time on social media than in all their years of school.
- **Cost to Concentration:** Simply receiving a **notification** — even without opening it — drains **mental energy** because the brain waits for the **reward** of checking the message. Students who keep their phones nearby while studying perform worse than those who put them in another room.

### 3. Reading Text Summary (`4.4`: *"The Tricks Behind Sale Events"*, pp. 301–302)
- **Three Psychological Sales Tricks:**
  1. **Loss Aversion (`كراهية الخسارة` — Kahneman & Tversky):** When a 400-pound jacket has a sign *"Was 800 LE, Now 400 LE, Save 50%!"*, your brain tells you *"If I don't buy it, I will lose the chance to save 400 pounds."* Not buying feels like a **loss**.
  2. **Scarcity & Social Proof (`خلق الإلحاح`):** Phrases like *"Only 3 left in stock!"* and seeing crowds buying make you fear missing out.
  3. **Countdown Clocks (`ساعات العد التنازلي`):** Force shoppers to decide quickly before thinking carefully.
- **Surprising Research Finding:** People who shop during sales **actually spend more money overall** than people who do not. A sale is not designed to save you money; it is designed to make you spend more and feel happy about it.

## Part 3: Section B Grammar Focus — The Present Perfect Continuous Tense & Tense Comparisons (pp. 293–294 & 307–308)

### 1. The Present Perfect Continuous Tense (`have / has + been + V-ing`)
- **Core Usages:**
  1. **An action that started in the past and is STILL continuing now** (with `for`, `since`, `all + day/morning/night/evening`, `How long`, `for ... now`, `since ... still`):
     - *It **has been raining** since 2 o'clock.* / *He **has been studying** English all evening.*
  2. **An action that lasted for a period in the past, recently stopped, and left a visible present explanation/symptom:**
     - *Marwan is very tired. He **has been studying** his lessons all day.*
     - *Her hands are covered in flour because she **has been baking** bread all morning.*

### 2. When You MUST Use Present Perfect Simple (`have/has + P.P.`) Instead of Continuous!
| Golden Rule | Wrong ($\times$) | Correct ($\checkmark$) |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Exact Number / Quantity / Frequency is stated** (`3 cups`, `5 emails`, `twice`, `How many / How much`) | $\times$ *I have been drinking 3 cups of coffee.* | $\checkmark$ ***I have drunk** 3 cups of coffee.* |
| **2. Stative Verbs (`أفعال الحالة والملكية والإدراك والمشاعر`):** `know, own, have (possess), love, hate, believe, understand, be` | $\times$ *Mona has been owning / knowing...* | $\checkmark$ ***Mona has owned** a car for 5 years.*<br>$\checkmark$ ***I have known** him for 10 years.* |
| **3. Instantaneous / Completed Result Focus** (`finish, break, lose, arrive, stop`) | $\times$ *Someone has been dropping the vase (it's broken).* | $\checkmark$ ***Someone has dropped** the vase.* |

### 3. Present Perfect (`have/has + P.P.`) vs. Past Simple (`Past Verb`) (p. 307)
- **Present Perfect:** Connected to the present (effect still holds, or time period is unfinished like `today / this morning` before noon, or person still works there): *Mona **has worked** at this company for three years (She still works there).*
- **Past Simple:** Finished in the past with no link to the present (or with `yesterday, last..., in 2018, when I was in Luxor`): *Mona **worked** at that company for three years (She doesn't work there now).*

## Part 4: Section C Writing Skills — Fronting (`التقديم للتوكيد`) & Opinion Article (pp. 313–314)

### 1. Fronting (`التقديم في الجمل الرسمية`, pp. 313–314)
- **Rule:** Instead of starting with `Subject + Verb`, move a **Time/Place, Result/Cause, or Contrast phrase** to the **very beginning of the sentence**, followed immediately by a **comma (`,`)** before the subject:
  $$\text{Fronted Phrase} + \mathbf{,} + \text{Subject} + \text{Verb} + \dots$$
- **Three Types of Fronting:**
  1. **Fronting a Time/Place Phrase:** *Food waste is a serious problem in Egypt.* $\rightarrow$ ***In Egypt,** food waste is a serious problem.*
  2. **Fronting a Result/Cause Phrase (`Because of / As a result of / Due to`):** *Bread goes bad quickly as a result of poor storage.* $\rightarrow$ ***As a result of poor storage,** bread goes bad quickly.*
  3. **Fronting for Contrast (`Unlike / In contrast to`):** *Egypt wastes most food at home, unlike richer countries.* $\rightarrow$ ***Unlike richer countries,** Egypt wastes most food at home.*

### 2. Structure of an Opinion Article (`مقال الرأي`, p. 314)
1. **Headline:** Short, catchy title showing your opinion.
2. **Introduction:** Give the topic and state your main opinion clearly.
3. **Body Paragraph 1:** First reason + example/evidence (`In my opinion, ... / I strongly believe that ...`).
4. **Body Paragraph 2:** Second reason + example/evidence.
5. **Concession:** Mention one point from the other side and refute/balance it (`Some people argue that... / While it is true that... / However, ...`).
6. **Conclusion:** Restate your opinion in new words and end with a strong final sentence (`In conclusion, / To sum up, / All in all,`).
