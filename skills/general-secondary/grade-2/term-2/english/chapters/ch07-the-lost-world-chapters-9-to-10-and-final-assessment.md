## Section 1: Story Text and Literary Analysis — Chapters 9 to 10 and Final Assessment

### Chapter Summaries

**Chapter 9: The Hidden Way Home**
After winning the battle for the plateau, the explorers search for a way to return to the world below. The bridge that brought them up has been destroyed, so they are trapped. They discover a secret cave — a hidden way — that leads them safely down from the plateau to the river and back to civilization.

**Chapter 10: Proof Before the World**
Back in London, the explorers face skepticism again. The scientific community does not believe Challenger's claims about living dinosaurs on a lost plateau. At a public meeting in Queen's Hall, Challenger presents undeniable proof: a living pterodactyl caught from the lost world. The creature flies in the hall, frightening the audience but convincing them of the truth. Challenger is recognized as a hero, and the explorers are celebrated. However, Malone discovers that Gladys married another man while he was away.

### Final Assessment — The Whole Novel (Chapters 1–10)

| Element | Summary |
|---------|---------|
| **Plot** | Challenger claims a lost plateau with prehistoric creatures; a team goes there; they survive dangers, fight ape-men, find a way home, and prove the truth in London. |
| **Main Characters** | Edward Malone (journalist, brave), Professor Challenger (bold scientist), Professor Summerlee (skeptic turned believer), Lord John Roxton (experienced hunter), Gladys Hungerton (Malone's love). |
| **Setting** | London; the Amazon; the lost plateau with dinosaurs and ape-men. |
| **Conflict** | Science vs. skepticism; humans vs. prehistoric beasts; explorers vs. ape-men. |
| **Theme** | Courage, scientific discovery, cooperation, and the need for proof. |
| **Ending** | Challenger is vindicated with the pterodactyl; Malone is brave but heartbroken; Roxton and Malone plan another adventure. |

### Character Development — Full Novel

| Character | Beginning | End | Change |
|-----------|-----------|-----|--------|
| **Edward Malone** | Unconfident reporter, rejected by Gladys | Brave hero, but Gladys married another | From seeking approval to acting with real courage |
| **Professor Challenger** | Mocked scientist with no proof | Recognized hero with the pterodactyl | From rejected to celebrated |
| **Professor Summerlee** | Totally skeptical of Challenger | Believes and respects Challenger | From doubt to recognition |
| **Lord John Roxton** | Skilled hunter and leader | Hero of the plateau battle | Stays brave and loyal throughout |

### Literary Themes — Full Novel

- **Courage:** Malone's growth from fear to bravery.
- **Scientific discovery vs. skepticism:** Challenger must prove his claims with real evidence.
- **Cooperation:** The explorers and the Accala tribe work together against the ape-men.
- **Justice and recognition:** Challenger finally receives the respect he deserves.

### [JRE] Question 7 — Justify, Reasoning & Evidence — Is Challenger a hero by the end of the novel?

**Justify:** Yes. Challenger is a hero because he proved his claims with real evidence and gained recognition after years of mockery.

**Reasoning:** A hero acts with courage and achieves something valuable. Challenger brought back proof (the pterodactyl) and convinced the scientific world that the lost world is real.

**Evidence:** At Queen's Hall, Challenger presents a living pterodactyl; the audience is convinced; the explorers become celebrated heroes.

**Step-by-Step Solution:**
Step 1: Recall Challenger's position at the end of Chapter 10.
Step 2: Identify whether he is a hero (he presented real proof).
Step 3: Provide reasoning (hero = courage + valuable achievement).
Step 4: Cite evidence (pterodactyl at Queen's Hall; audience convinced; explorers celebrated).

### [JRE] Question 8 — Justify, Reasoning & Evidence — Is the ending fair for Malone?

**Justify:** Partly no. Malone becomes a brave hero, but he loses Gladys to another man, which is a personal loss despite his public success.

**Reasoning:** Fairness means both professional and personal rewards match effort. Malone gains courage and respect, but his personal hope (marrying Gladys) is destroyed.

**Evidence:** Malone is celebrated as a hero after the pterodactyl proof, but he discovers Gladys married another man while he was away.

**Step-by-Step Solution:**
Step 1: Recall Malone's situation at the end (heroic but heartbroken).
Step 2: Identify whether the ending is fair (partly no — personal loss).
Step 3: Provide reasoning (professional success does not replace personal loss).
Step 4: Cite evidence (celebrated hero; Gladys married another man).

### [JRE] Question 9 — Justify, Reasoning & Evidence — Does the pterodactyl prove that Challenger was right?

**Justify:** Yes. The living pterodactyl is physical, undeniable evidence that prehistoric creatures exist on the lost plateau.

**Reasoning:** Scientific claims require evidence. A living prehistoric creature shown in public is stronger evidence than words, drawings, or bones.

**Evidence:** Challenger presents the pterodactyl at Queen's Hall; the creature flies in the hall; the audience is convinced of Challenger's truth.

**Step-by-Step Solution:**
Step 1: Recall the proof Challenger presented in Chapter 10.
Step 2: Identify whether it proves his claim (yes, it is living evidence).
Step 3: Provide reasoning (living creature > words or bones).
Step 4: Cite evidence (pterodactyl at Queen's Hall; audience convinced).
