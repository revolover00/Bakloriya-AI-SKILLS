### 3. FOCUS on Vocabulary — Confusable Words (`2.1`, p. 99)
1. **`boundary` vs. `border` vs. `limit`:**
   - **boundary** (حد فاصل بين ملكيتين أو حد/قاعدة في علاقة شخصية): *The fence marks the boundary between the two houses* / *set clear boundaries between work life and personal life*.
   - **border** (الحدود السياسية الدولية بين دولتين): *They crossed the border into Canada*.
   - **limit** (حد أقصى لا يُسمح بتجاوزه): *speed limit / a limit of two bags per passenger*.
2. **`interfere` vs. `intrude`:**
   - **interfere** (يتدخل فيما لا يعنيه أو يعرقل/يؤثر سلبًا): *I never interfere in matters that don't concern me* / *The loud music interfered with my concentration*.
   - **intrude** (يقتحم مكانًا أو يتطفل على الخصوصية بلا إذن): *intrude into the celebrity's private home / intrude on my privacy*.
3. **Uses of `average`:**
   - **Noun (مُعدَّل إحصائي / حد وسطي):** *The average speed was 120 km/h* / *On average, people work 40 hours per week*.
   - **Adjective (متوسط / عادي):** *The food was average, nothing to write home about*.
   - **Verb (يحسب معدل شيء / يبلغ متوسطه):** *averaging about 12 meters*.

### 4. Listening Text Summary (`2.1`: *"Screen Time and Young People — Research Report"*, pp. 96–97)
- **Social Media & Self-Image:** Teenagers spend an average of **7 hours a day** looking at screens (excluding schoolwork). Pressure for "likes" and negative comparison on Instagram/TikTok increase **anxiety, loneliness, and depression**, causing some teens to **withdraw** from real-world social activities.
- **Gaming — Passion or Addiction?:** Moderate gaming develops **problem-solving skills, hand-eye coordination, and teamwork**. However, when gaming becomes **addictive** and **interferes** with sleep, schoolwork, and relationships, it becomes harmful; the **WHO officially recognized "gaming disorder" as a mental health condition in 2019**.
- **Stepping Away & Finding Balance:** Outdoor activities, face-to-face socializing, and creative hobbies offer benefits screens cannot **replicate**. Setting clear **boundaries** (e.g., avoiding phones 1 hour before sleep, screen-free family meals) helps youth feel in control and less anxious.

### 3. Reading Text Summary (`2.2`: *"Creative Arts Are Good for Your Brain and Your Soul"*, pp. 110–111)
1. **The Psychological Benefits of "Flow":** Psychologists call complete absorption in a meaningful task **"flow"** (time slows down, worries fade). Just **30 minutes of creative engagement per day** measurably reduces levels of the stress hormone **cortisol**.
2. **Creative Hobbies and Cognitive Growth:** Neuroscientists found that drawing, playing an instrument, or writing activates multiple brain regions, strengthens **neural connections**, and improves **cognitive flexibility** because it demands that the brain **produce rather than merely consume**.
3. **Self-Expression & Cultural Identity:** Art provides a safe, non-judgmental **arena** for **authentic self-expression**. In Egypt, traditional forms like **calligraphy, arabesque design, music, and storytelling** connect youth to their heritage in a contemporary way.
4. **Collaborative Skills & Resilience Through Failure:** A school orchestra, drama production, or street art collective teaches youth to listen and work toward a shared goal. Creative arts also teach youth to **embrace failure** (a painting that doesn't turn out as planned is a lesson, not a failure; a musician who plays a wrong note learns more than from a perfect **rehearsal**). Research by **Arts Council England** showed youth in creative arts have greater emotional resilience and volunteer more.

## Part 3: Section A Grammar Focus — Gerunds (`V + ing`) & Infinitives (`to + inf.`) (pp. 102–104 & 116–118)

### 1. Verbs Followed by `Gerund (V + ing)` vs. `to + Infinitive`
| Category | Verbs / Expressions | Important Rules & Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Verbs followed by `V + ing`** | `admit (to), appreciate, avoid, consider, delay, deny, discuss, dislike, enjoy, escape, fancy, finish, go (activities), imagine, involve, keep, mind, miss, postpone, practice, recall, report, resist, risk, spend, stand, suggest` | • **Passive Gerund:** `being + P.P.` (*He avoids **being met** by bad people*).<br>• **`go` rule:** `go + V-ing` for sports/activities (*go swimming*), but `go + adj` means "become worse" (`go bad, go blind, go wrong, go sour`). |
| **2. Verbs followed by `to + inf.` directly** | `agree, aim, appear, attempt, choose, dare, decide, determine, fail, hope, learn, long, manage, mean, offer, plan, pretend, prove, refuse, seem, struggle, undertake, want, wish` | *Our team **managed to win** the match yesterday.* |
| **3. Verbs followed by `Object + to + inf.`** | `advise, allow, cause, demand, encourage, force, get, invite, offer, order, persuade, remind, request, teach, tell, train, urge, warn` | *The doctor **advised the patient to drink** more water.*<br>*(Note: `advise / allow / permit / encourage / forbid` take `V-ing` if there is NO object!).* |
| **4. Verbs taking `to + inf.` OR `Object + to + inf.`** | `ask, beg, choose, expect, help, need, promise, want` | *I **expect to receive** the package* / *I **need you to fix** my car.* |

### 2. Expressions Ending in Preposition `to` + `V + ing` (Critical Exam Trap!)
When `to` is a **preposition** (part of a phrasal verb/expression), it is followed by **`V + ing` or a Noun** (NEVER `to + inf.`!):
- `look forward to` (يتطلع إلى), `object to` (يعترض على), `(be/get) used to` (معتاد على), `(be/get) accustomed to` (معتاد على), `in addition to` (بالإضافة إلى), `with a view to` (بهدف), `devote ... to` / `dedicate oneself to` (يكرس لـ), `contribute to` (يسهم في), `stick to` (يلتزم بـ), `adjust to / adapt to` (يتأقلم مع), `confess to` (يعترف بـ), `take to` (يعتاد على/يحب), `lead to` (يؤدي إلى), `when it comes to` (عندما يتعلق الأمر بـ), `close to` (قريب من), `react to` (يتفاعل مع), `addicted to` (مدمن على).
- **Other Expressions + `V + ing`:** `(be) busy`, `It's (not) worth`, `It's no good`, `It's no use`, `How/What about`, `what's the use of`, `as well as`, `have difficulty (in)`, `have trouble`, `can't help` (لا يتمالك نفسه عن), `can't stand` (لا يتحمل), `feel like` (يرغب في), `there is no point in` (لا جدوى من), `don't mind` (لا يمانع).

---

### 3. Verbs Followed by `V + ing` OR `to + inf.`

#### A. With NO Change in Meaning:
- `hate, like, love, prefer, start, begin, continue, intend, bother`.
- **Exception:** If `start / begin / continue` is itself in a **continuous tense (`starting / beginning`)**, follow it with **`to + inf.` only**: *Take your umbrella; it's **starting to rain** (NOT: starting raining)*.

#### B. With a CLEAR Change in Meaning (High-Yield Exam Table!):
| Verb | `+ Gerund (V + ing)` | `+ to + Infinitive` |
|---|---|---|
| **`stop`** | **Quit an action completely or pause doing it:**<br>*He **stopped taking** sugar in his tea.* | **Pause walking/doing something IN ORDER TO do another action:**<br>*On his way to the office, he **stopped to take** cash from the ATM.* |
| **`remember`** | **Do the action FIRST, then recall the memory later:**<br>*I **remember locking** the safe before leaving. I can clearly picture doing it.* | **Remember the duty FIRST, then do the action:**<br>*Please **remember to lock** the safe before you leave tonight.* |
| **`forget`** | **Did the action in the past, then forgot (or never forgot) the memory:**<br>*She will never **forget meeting** the President.* | **Forgot the duty, so the action did NOT happen:**<br>*She **forgot to meet** the client at the airport, so he took a taxi.* |
| **`regret`** | **Did something in the past and feel sorry about it now:**<br>*I **regret selling** my old car; the new one breaks down constantly.* | **Feel sorry that you have to announce bad news now:**<br>*We **regret to inform** you that your application has been rejected.* |
| **`try`** | **Experiment with an easy method to see if it works (يجرّب ليرى النتيجة):**<br>*If you have a headache, **try drinking** more water.* | **Make a hard effort to do something difficult (يحاول ببذل مجهود، وغالبًا يفشل):**<br>*The doctor **tried to save** the patient, but the injury was fatal.* |
