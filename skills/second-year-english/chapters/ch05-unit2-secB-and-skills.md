### 3. Listening Text Summary (`2.3`: *"The Benefits of Sports Pyramid: From Foundation to Peak"*, p. 128)
- **The "Movement Crisis":** The WHO recommends **60 minutes** of medium-to-hard physical activity daily for youth aged 5–17. However, a **2024 survey by the Egyptian Ministry of Youth and Sports** found that **fewer than 1 in 3 secondary school students** met recommended weekly activity levels due to screen time and academic pressure — creating a **"movement crisis"**.
- **Psychological & Social Gains:** Beyond cardiovascular health and stronger bones, team-sport participants show **higher self-esteem, better emotional regulation, and greater social competence** than non-sporting peers. Sport teaches youth how to **lose gracefully**, support teammates, and **persevere** through difficulty — skills that transfer directly into academic and professional life.
- **Access & Long-Term Participation:** Cost, location, and lack of **facilities/resources** limit access for some youth. Crucially, **enjoyment is the strongest predictor of long-term participation into adulthood**: when youth are forced into competitive sport they dislike or when failure is treated harshly, they **disengage**.

### 3. Reading Text Summary (`2.4`: *"Not Exactly a Relaxing Day"*, pp. 142–143)
- **Literary Personal Narrative set along the Nile:**
  - **Initial attitude:** The narrator believed ideal leisure meant staying home in complete stillness on the sofa watching a series. His friend **insisted** he join a cycling trip along the Nile.
  - **First hour (dawn):** The air was cool, the road quiet, and the Nile **shimmered**; cycling felt effortless.
  - **Turning point (as the morning wore on):** The road became less shaded, the sun grew hotter, his legs ached, and he **struggled to keep up** with his experienced friend. He seriously considered giving up and calling a taxi.
  - **Climax & Resolution:** Two things stopped him: **pride** (unwillingness to admit defeat) and **genuine curiosity** about whether he could finish. Taking short breaks and focusing on one **pedal stroke** at a time, he cycled much farther than expected. Sitting beside the Nile afterward with deep physical tiredness, he felt a profound **sense of achievement** and realized the best moments come from **effort and pushing past the point where you wanted to stop**.

## Part 3: Section B Grammar Focus — Advanced Gerund & Infinitive Rules (pp. 133–135 & 148–149)

### 1. Additional Uses of `Gerund (V + ing)`
1. **As a Subject at the start of a sentence (treated as SINGULAR!):**
   - ***Getting** up early **strengthens** your body and **makes** you active.*
   - ***Reading** books and stories **is** interesting.*
2. **After ALL Prepositions (`without, after, before, since, by, in, on, of, for`):**
   - *He left the shop **without paying**, so he was accused **of stealing**.*
3. **After Time Conjunctions (`After, Before, While, When, Since`) when the subject is omitted (same subject in both clauses):**
   - ***After finishing** my homework, I helped my brother.*
4. **After Possessive Adjectives (`my, your, his, her, our, their`) or `'s`:**
   - *I appreciate **your helping** me.* / *Do you mind **my opening** the window?*

### 2. Purpose Clauses & `too ... to` / `adjective + enough to`
1. **Expressing Purpose:**
   - **Same subject:** `to / in order to / so as to + inf.` (Negative: `in order not to / so as not to + inf.`).
   - **Different subjects (or same subject with modal):** `so that / in order that / in the hope that + Subject + can/will (present) OR could/would (past) + inf.`:
     *I studied hard **so that** I **could** get high marks.*
2. **`too + adjective + (for object) + to + inf.` (Negative meaning = أكثر من اللازم لدرجة ألا):**
   - *The box is **too heavy to carry**.* *(Note: do NOT write `to carry it` or `to be carried` when the object of the infinitive is the subject of the sentence!).*
   - **Special Note on `busy`:** `busy + V-ing` (*He's too busy **attending** the meetings* = he is attending them right now), vs. `too busy to + inf.` (*He's **too busy to attend** the meetings* = he cannot attend them).
3. **`adjective + enough + (for object) + to + inf.` (Positive meaning = بدرجة كافية):**
   - *My brother is **clever enough to pass** the test.* / *The car **isn't cheap enough to buy**.*

### 3. Special Uses of `to + inf.` vs. Bare Infinitive (`inf.` without `to`)
- **Use `to + inf.` after:**
  1. `would like / would love / would prefer` (*I **would like to see** the manager*).
  2. Emotion adjectives (`happy, glad, sorry, surprised, amazed`): *She was **sorry to miss** the conference*.
  3. `only + to + inf.` to express a disappointing/unexpected result: *He came **only to meet** his friend*.
  4. Ordinal numbers & superlatives (`the first, the second, the last, the next, the best`): *He was **the first to come** to the party*.
- **Use Bare Infinitive (`inf.` without `to`) after:**
  1. `had better`, `would rather`, `let + obj + inf.`, `make + obj + inf.` *(Note: in Passive, `be made to + inf.` takes `to`!)*, `help + obj + inf. / to + inf.`.
     - *Watch out:* If `would rather` is followed by a **different subject**, use **Past Simple** (for present/future: *I'd rather Ali **went** to bed early*) or **Past Perfect** (for past: *I'd rather Ali **had gone** to bed early yesterday*).
  2. Modal verbs (`can, will, must, may, might, should`).
  3. Subjunctive after `suggest / recommend + (that) + Subject + (should) + bare inf.`:
     *He suggested that she **help** the children with their homework.*

### 4. More Verbs Changing Meaning with `V + ing` vs. `to + inf.`
| Verb | `+ Gerund (V + ing)` | `+ Infinitive (to + inf. / bare inf.)` |
|---|---|---|
| **`go on`** | **Continue doing the SAME action:**<br>*She **went on talking** for hours.* | **Move on to a NEW action after finishing the first:**<br>*After finishing the report, she **went on to type** some letters.* |
| **`mean`** | **Involve / require as a necessary result (يشمل / يتضمن):**<br>*Finding a job **means attending** many interviews.* | **Intend (يقصد / ينوي):**<br>*He **means to find** a job abroad.* |
| **`need`** | **With an inanimate subject = Passive meaning (`= need to be + P.P.`):**<br>*My shoes are quite dull. They **need polishing** (`= need to be polished`).* | **With a person = Active obligation (يجب أن):**<br>*I **need to polish** them.* |
| **Verbs of Perception (`see, hear, listen to, watch`)** | **`+ Object + V + ing` = Saw/heard PART of the action in progress:**<br>*I watched my father **painting** the living room.* *(Note: after `can/could see/hear`, ALWAYS use `V-ing`!).* | **`+ Object + bare inf.` (without `to`) = Saw/heard the WHOLE action from start to finish:**<br>*I watched my father **paint** the living room.* |

## Part 4: Section C Writing Skills — Opinion Paragraph & Diary Entry (pp. 155–156)

### 1. Structure of an Opinion Paragraph (`فقرة إبداء الرأي`)
1. **Opinion (Claim):** State your stance on the topic clearly in the opening sentence.
2. **Reason:** Give a logical reason to support your opinion.
3. **Example / Evidence:** Add real-life examples or explanations (*For example, people can play sports..., try hobbies such as drawing...*).
4. **Conclusion:** Restate your opinion in a different way (*In my opinion, everyone should use their free time wisely...*).

### 2. Structure of a Reflective Diary Entry (`مذكرة يومية`, p. 156)
A diary entry records daily experiences, reflections, and emotions in an honest, personal space using **narrative tenses**:
1. **Opening (Date & Salutation — `Dear Diary,`):** Set the scene (where you were, what the weather/mood was like).
2. **The Story:** Recount the main events in chronological order using narrative tenses (first positive hour, then rising difficulty).
3. **Turning Point:** Describe the moment you most wanted to stop, using **Past Continuous** to show what was happening around you and what kept you going.
4. **Reflection:** Describe how it felt when you finally stopped (using vivid detail or a simile for exhaustion and satisfaction).
5. **The Ending (The Message):** State what this experience taught you about yourself or life.
6. **Final Sentence:** End with a memorable philosophical or personal line (*"I am ending this day feeling happy, grateful, and refreshed."*).
