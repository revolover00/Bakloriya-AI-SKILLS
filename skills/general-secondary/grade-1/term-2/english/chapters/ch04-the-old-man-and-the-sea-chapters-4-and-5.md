## Section 1: Story Text and Literary Analysis — Chapters 4 to 5

### Chapter Summaries

**Chapter 4: Santiago and the Marlin**
Santiago feels the marlin pulling his skiff far out to sea. The fish is enormous — two feet longer than the boat. Santiago holds the line with all his strength, his back aching and his hands cut by the fishing line. He talks to himself and to the fish, showing his loneliness and his respect for the marlin as a worthy opponent.

**Chapter 5: The Struggle Continues**
The struggle continues for hours. Santiago eats raw tuna to keep his strength. He admires the marlin's beauty and nobility, calling it his "brother." He knows he must kill the fish to survive, but he feels sorrow and respect at the same time. The marlin circles the boat, and Santiago slowly brings it closer, suffering from cramps and exhaustion.

### Character Analysis — Santiago in Chapters 4–5

| Aspect | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Physical suffering** | Back pain, cut hands, cramps, exhaustion from holding the line |
| **Mental strength** | Talks to himself to stay awake and focused; refuses to give up |
| **Respect for the marlin** | Calls the fish his brother; admires its strength, beauty, and nobility |
| **Determination** | Will kill the fish even if it kills him; "I will fight until I die" |
| **Resourcefulness** | Eats raw tuna; manages the line carefully despite pain |

### Literary Themes

- **Struggle and endurance:** Santiago's long battle with the marlin tests his physical and mental limits
- **Respect for nature:** Santiago sees the marlin as a noble creature, not just prey
- **Isolation:** Santiago is alone at sea, talking to himself and the fish
- **Man vs. nature:** The old man's battle against a powerful fish represents humanity's struggle against natural forces

### [JRE] Question 7 — Justify, Reasoning & Evidence — Is Santiago's struggle with the marlin mainly physical or mental?

**Justify:** It is both physical and mental. The physical struggle is holding the line and enduring pain; the mental struggle is staying focused, hopeful, and determined.

**Reasoning:** Santiago's body suffers (cut hands, back pain, cramps), but his mind keeps him going through self-talk, memories, and respect for the fish. Without mental strength, he would give up.

**Evidence:** Santiago talks to himself to stay awake (Chapter 5), remembers past successes to build confidence, and admires the marlin despite his pain (Chapter 4–5).

**Step-by-Step Solution:**
Step 1: Recall Santiago's physical and mental experiences during the struggle.
Step 2: Identify whether it is physical, mental, or both (both).
Step 3: Provide reasoning (body endures pain; mind provides determination).
Step 4: Cite evidence (self-talk; memories; admiring the marlin).

### [JRE] Question 8 — Justify, Reasoning & Evidence — Does Santiago respect nature?

**Justify:** Yes, Santiago respects nature deeply. He sees the marlin as a noble creature, admires the sea, and treats the fish with dignity even while trying to kill it.

**Reasoning:** Respect for nature means recognizing the value and dignity of living creatures, not just using them. Santiago's admiration for the marlin shows this respect.

**Evidence:** Santiago calls the marlin his "brother," admires its beauty and strength, and feels sorrow at the thought of killing it (Chapter 4–5).

**Step-by-Step Solution:**
Step 1: Recall Santiago's attitude toward the marlin and the sea.
Step 2: Identify whether he respects nature (yes).
Step 3: Provide reasoning (recognizing the fish's nobility and dignity).
Step 4: Cite evidence (calling the marlin "brother"; admiring its beauty; feeling sorrow).
